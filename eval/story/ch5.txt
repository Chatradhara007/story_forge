Tobias arrived the morning after the storm, or so he told the reporter from the Wick Gazette who came out on Thursday to write about the keeper who had kept her light burning through the worst gale in memory.

The reporter admired the Merrow, which was moored at the landing with its blue hull freshly scrubbed. "Fine boat," he said.

Ines sat in the kitchen with her wrist in a clean white bandage and answered his questions as briefly as she could. "In my eight years as keeper," she said, "I've never let it go dark."

"And your family? Your brother?"

"Tobias, my younger brother," she said, "is staying for a while."

Outside, Bramble barked at the gulls from the rocks, the way he always did when strangers came.

The reporter had one more question. "They say Silas Rudd rowed out to the Gullstone on the night of the storm to help you. Is that true?"

Ines shrugged. "On the night of the storm, Silas Rudd rowed out to the Gullstone and sat with me in the lamp room until dawn," she said. "He's a good man. Put that in your paper."

Pip, whose ankle had healed by the end of the week, showed the reporter his notebook of storm clouds.
