Chapter Four

Walter Alcott died on Thursday, a little after six in the morning, with the man from bed five holding his hand and still losing at the card game. Priya signed the certificate herself and then went and stood in the ambulance bay for ten minutes in the cold.

The department had all twelve beds full again by the time she came back in. Tom was on the phone to bed management, threatening someone with Marguerite.

Felix was waiting for her with two cups of tea. He had stopped apologising to the vending machine. He pushed his blond hair out of his eyes and handed her a cup.

"I'm sorry about Mr Alcott," he said.

"So am I." She drank the tea. "He liked you. He said you had a surgeon's hands."

"He said I had terrible hair."

"That too."

At seven Marguerite came down with the day manager and the week's figures. Priya's older sister read the numbers out like a verdict: waiting times, ambulance delays, the cost of the generator that had failed.

"They'll close you," Marguerite said. "Not this year. But they'll close you."

Priya went home at nine and slept until four in the afternoon. When she woke Leo was sitting on the end of the bed, reading a comic and eating cereal out of the box.

"Aunt Marguerite says you're stubborn," he said.

"Aunt Marguerite is right," said Priya.
