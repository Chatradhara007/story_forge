Chapter One

The emergency department at St Brennan's sat at the bottom of Calder Street, where the hill ran out and the river began. It had eight beds, two of them always broken, and on a Monday night in November every one of them was full.

Dr Priya Sandoval had been running the night shift for six years. She was thirty-eight, small and quick, with green eyes and short black hair she cut herself in the staff toilet when it got in the way. A thin scar split her right eyebrow, a souvenir from a drunk with a bottle in her first year. She was left-handed, and the nurses had long ago learned to lay her instruments out backwards.

Her new resident was Felix Hart, twenty-seven years old and terrified. He had red hair that stood up no matter what he did to it, and he apologised to the vending machine when it ate his coins.

"Bed four," Priya told him. "Walter Alcott, eighty-one, fell in his kitchen. Deaf in his left ear, so talk to the right one."

The charge nurse, Tom Okafor, was fifty-two and built like a door. He had worked at St Brennan's longer than anyone, and he had opinions about everything, most of them correct.

Before midnight Priya rang home. Her son Leo was nine and supposed to be asleep. He was not.

"Aunt Marguerite let me watch the film," he said.

Marguerite Doyle was Priya's older sister, the hospital's administrator, and the only person in the building Tom Okafor was afraid of. She was sixty, grey-haired, and she had taken Leo for the week without being asked, which was how Marguerite did everything.

At four in the morning Priya took out her father's brass pocket watch to time a patient's breathing. It had stopped working years ago, and she had it mended every spring anyway. It was the only thing of his she had.
