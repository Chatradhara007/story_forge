Chapter Three

The blackout came on Wednesday night, a little after two. The whole of Calder Street went dark at once, and then the hospital's generator coughed, caught, and failed.

For eleven minutes the emergency department ran on torches and the battery packs of the monitors. Priya walked from bed to bed with a torch in her teeth, counting breaths. Tom sat at the desk with his cast propped on a box and phoned every number he knew, one-handed, until an electrician answered.

Felix kept his head. He was the one who remembered the oxygen cylinders in the old store room, and the one who ran for them.

When the lights came back, Priya found him sitting on the floor of the store room, shaking.

"You did well," she said. She took her father's brass pocket watch out of her pocket and pressed it into his hand. "Here. You'll need something to time breaths with that doesn't need a battery. It's yours now. Don't lose it."

Felix tried to give it back. She would not take it.

At the end of the shift Walter Alcott called her over. His face was a bad colour in the grey light.

"I'm tired, Doctor," he said. "I think I'm more tired than I've ever been."

She stayed with him for a while. She was not sure afterwards whether he had heard anything she said, or only seen her mouth moving on his good side.
