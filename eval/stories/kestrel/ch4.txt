Chapter Four

Twenty years earlier, Yusra Adeyemi was twenty-five and a junior officer on the freighter Calloway, and she had never been in charge of anything more complicated than a cargo hold.

She remembered the coolant fire very clearly. The alarm, the white fog of the coolant, the sleeve of her uniform melting onto her left forearm as she pulled the engineer out of the compartment. She remembered the medic who dressed the burn and told her it would scar, and how little she had cared.

She remembered, too, the day her sister phoned to say she had had a baby boy and was calling him Bastian. Yusra had been on the far side of the system, three weeks from home, and she had cried in a storage locker where nobody could see.

On Kestrel, twenty years later, she woke from that memory in the dark of the hub. The regulator was whining. Her son Bastian was asleep in the chair opposite, his newly bare chin on his chest, a wrench still in his hand.

She watched him sleep for a while. Then she got up, very quietly, and went to look at the oxygen figures again. She caught her reflection in the black glass of the viewport: an old woman, she thought, with tired dark brown eyes and a scar on her arm, keeping watch over a failing station.

The figures had not improved. Twelve days to the drop.
