The Gullstone Light stood on a knuckle of black rock two miles off Port Wick, and Ines Calloway had kept it burning for twenty years.

She was fifty-two that spring, lean and weathered, with grey eyes that the fishermen said could spot a sail through fog. Every evening at dusk she climbed the ninety-one steps of the tower, trimmed the wick, and wound the clockwork that turned the great lens.

Her older brother Tobias wrote to her twice a year from the mainland. His letters were short and smelled of pipe smoke. This year's letter said only that he was tired of the city and might come out to the rock before the autumn gales.

Below the tower, in the keeper's cottage, lived Ines's old sheepdog, Bramble, who was nearly deaf and slept by the stove. Ines talked to Bramble more than she talked to anyone in Port Wick.

On the last day of the month, the supply boat brought flour, lamp oil, and a boy. His name was Pip Aldane. The harbourmaster had sent him out to learn the work, since Ines would not be young forever. Pip was small and freckled and asked a great many questions.

"Is it true the light has never gone out?" he asked on his first night.

"Not on my watch," said Ines.
