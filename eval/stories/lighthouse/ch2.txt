Silas Rudd, the harbourmaster of Port Wick, was a broad man with a white beard that reached the second button of his coat. He came out to the Gullstone in the Merrow, the harbour's little red-painted launch, to see how the boy was settling in.

"He'll do," Ines told him. "He's quick with the lens and he doesn't complain about the stairs."

Rudd stroked his beard and looked out at the horizon. "Glass is falling," he said. "There's weather coming. A big one, maybe."

Three days before the storm, Tobias Calloway arrived on the mail boat. He was a tall man with dark hair going grey at the temples, and he carried his bag in his right hand. He had lost his left hand to a winch accident at sea many years ago, and he kept the empty sleeve pinned neatly to his coat.

Ines met him at the landing. They did not embrace; the Calloways never did. But she took his bag, and he let her, which was the same thing.

That evening Tobias sat at the kitchen table and watched Pip polish the brass fittings. "You've got yourself an apprentice," he said.

"I've got myself a lot of questions," said Ines. "The apprentice came with them."
