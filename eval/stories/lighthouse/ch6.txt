Autumn came in quietly. Since the storm on Friday, the sea had been as flat as a slate roof.

Ines moved into the village for the winter, as keepers sometimes did when an assistant could mind the light. She took a room above the chandler's shop in Port Wick and went out to the rock every other day.

Twelve-year-old Pip was left in charge of the tower on the days she was away. He took the job very seriously. Each evening he climbed the ninety-one steps, and each evening Ines, on her visits, unlocked the lamp room with the old brass key and inspected his work.

Harbourmaster Rudd stopped by the chandler's shop most mornings. He had taken to rubbing his clean-shaven chin when he was thinking, a habit Ines found oddly comforting.

"The boy's doing well," Rudd said.

"He is," said Ines. "Better than I did at his age."

Mara wrote from the mainland to say she might visit again in spring. Tobias read the letter twice and put it in his pocket.
