Pip was ten years old, and he had never seen a real storm at sea. He kept a notebook of cloud shapes and showed it to anyone who would look.

The week before the storm was quiet, and sad in its own way. Bramble died in the night, curled by the stove where he had always slept. Ines buried him behind the cottage, under the thrift flowers, and did not speak for the rest of the day. Pip made a small cross from driftwood and did not ask any questions at all.

Two days later, Ines's niece Mara Calloway came out on the supply boat with a basket of apples and news from the mainland. Mara was Tobias's daughter, twenty years old, with a sharp laugh and no patience for lighthouses. She stayed one night and left on the morning tide.

That same afternoon, Tobias found the old brass key to the lamp room hanging on its nail. "You still lock it?" he asked.

"Every night," said Ines.

Tobias turned the key over in his fingers. Then, without a word, he walked to the edge of the rocks and threw it into the sea. "Nobody locks a lamp room anymore," he said. Ines said nothing, but that night she wedged the door shut with a chair.

Pip slipped on the wet steps that evening and sprained his ankle. Ines bound it tightly and told him to stay off his feet.
