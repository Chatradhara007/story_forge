Twelve years before the fire, Orla Venn was twenty-two and had never made anything worth selling.

She had come to Varrow as a girl of nineteen, three years before, with nothing but a satchel and a letter of introduction to a glass-singer. The glass-singer was Ysolde, who was very much alive then and not yet old, and who laughed at the letter and let her stay anyway.

"Listen to it," Ysolde would say, tapping a cooling bowl. "Glass tells you where it will break before it breaks." Orla listened. Her hands, in those days, were smooth, her palms unmarked by any burn or cut.

A young watchman named Aldous Crane walked the canal path every night and stopped at the window to watch them work. He was already tall and already stooping, and Ysolde teased him that he would grow into a question mark.

The winter Orla turned twenty-two, she tried to lift a gather of molten glass that was too heavy for her. The blowpipe slipped, and the hot iron laid open the skin of her right palm from the thumb to the little finger. Ysolde hummed over the wound until the pain went quiet, but the scar stayed. Orla never minded it. It was the night she decided she would have her own workshop one day.
