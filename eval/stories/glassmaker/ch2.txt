The fire began just after midnight.

Orla woke to the smell of it and ran down the stairs. The workshop was already full of light: all three furnaces were roaring, their doors thrown open, and flames were climbing the shelves of finished glass. She pressed her left palm, where the old scar ran, against her mouth and nose and pushed through the smoke.

Rook was at the water barrel, throwing bucket after bucket. In the orange light he looked older, and when Captain Crane arrived with the watch and called out "Marsh! Edwin Marsh!", the boy flinched as if he'd been struck. So that was the truth of it: Rook's real name was Edwin Marsh, the runaway son of a family who owed half the city money. He had been hiding in Orla's workshop for three years.

The green ledger burned to ash on the counter before anyone could reach it. The Sunstone lens slid from the bench and shattered on the stone floor into a hundred golden pieces.

Old Ysolde did not come down the stairs. They found her afterwards in her chair by the window, as if she had simply fallen asleep in the smoke. She was dead before dawn.

Out on the canal, Tom Fenner had been ferrying buckets from the far bank. His boat capsized when the roof beams fell into the water, and Tom Fenner drowned before anyone could reach him.

Tom Brisk, the captain's young clerk, stood at the edge of the crowd with his notebook and wrote down the names of the dead in careful letters.
